What happens in practice

You say:

“Jessica, order groceries for our five dinners.”

Jessica sends that request to the Guardian API server. That server is the brain behind the voice interface—it holds the meal plan, household rules, shopping logic, and approval ledger.

Jessica then responds:

“I found five dinners. I’m building one consolidated Walmart cart and excluding pantry staples.”

The shopper engine:

* Converts every meal into ingredients and quantities.
* Combines duplicates.
* Checks your pantry records.
* Applies household limits and preferred brands.
* Searches Walmart first.
* Sends missing or urgent items to DoorDash or Stater Bros.
* Does not buy anything yet.

Then Jessica says something like:

“Your Walmart order contains 31 items for approximately $127. Two substitutions are proposed. Ground sirloin replaces shaved steak, and frozen green beans replace fresh. Delivery is available between 4 and 6 PM. Say ‘review,’ ‘commit,’ or ‘cancel.’”

You say:

“Commit.”

Only then does the system place the order and write the result to the household ledger.

What must actually be running

There are three separate pieces:

1. Guardian API server
    The backend brain containing meals, shopping rules, approvals, and household data.
2. Jessica voice interface
    Captures your speech, sends it to the server, and reads the response aloud.
3. Retailer connector
    Builds or submits the Walmart/DoorDash cart.

The important reality: we know the Guardian server and Jessica concepts exist in your project, but we have not verified that the retailer connector is implemented. Until it is, Jessica may generate the cart correctly but still require you to open Walmart and add or approve the items manually.

So our first live test is not “buy groceries.” It is:

Start the Guardian server → ask Jessica for the five meals → verify she generates the correct consolidated cart → confirm she stops before purchasing.

Then we connect the cart to a retailer safely.
